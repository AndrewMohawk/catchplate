## Retention, publication and limits

The bundle goes through the existing exclusive public-file installer. At the
first real bridge-delivery request, the retained owner checks the actual
installation result and its two output pins. Later reads require identical
input/result/source bytes and the same original boot and deadline bounds.
Authentication never extends the startup, request, grant, checkpoint or UNIX
ceilings. Cleanup is permitted only after the same process proves terminal
cleanup; failed/partial attempts cannot be retried or adopted.

The existing installer guards its public writes with the original driver
window; it does not acquire a new inner-grant deadline argument. The retained
composition/result handoff and native consumers enforce their stricter grant
ceilings. Public files can therefore remain after a failed or expired attempt;
their existence is not release or credential-use authority.

Existing limits remain: 4 MiB runtime, 16 MiB versioned bundle and 128 MiB
aggregate retained source data. Policy is bounded to 64 KiB. History and
authority names must be distinct because the bridge credential map is flat.
Ordinary-service-owned `0400` histories are not automatically readable by the
bridge; a separate provisioner must supply its approved credential material.
